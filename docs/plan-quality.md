# Brief fidelity and architectural presentation

Customer problem: a homeowner's entered room targets produced unnecessarily
large rooms, missing primary furniture and weak interior compositions. The
editable plan and measured sheet also differed in drafting conventions and units.

Journey: describe rooms → generate a study → compare targets and unresolved
directions → review the shared plan → explore furnished rooms → play a tour.

Acceptance criteria:
- Preserve every requested room, floor and target without modifying the brief.
- Choose a compact feasible footprint; never stretch the last named room to
  consume spare floor depth. Retain connected circulation and stair apertures.
- Flag materially larger areas independently from unknown/mismatched directions.
- Place primary furniture where a checked route to each door remains available;
  show failed furnishing suggestions rather than silently claiming completion.
- Use consistent selected area units, restrained monochrome drawing linework,
  measured openings and recognizable shared furniture in sheets and the editor.
- Interior cameras must frame primary furnishings from a walkable location.
- Verify the user's Jaipur brief and three-storey Jaipur/Delhi across plan,
  3D, walking, tour playback and editable export; preserve review/save gates.

Quality measures: target-versus-generated room area, primary fixture presence,
door connectivity, room-visible camera framing, and no console or overflow errors.
These are bounded concept quality checks, not municipal or engineering approval.

North and local limits stay unknown unless supplied. Existing user drafts and
revisions must be backed up before replacement. No new AI processor or asset
service is introduced. Physical iPhone and spoken VoiceOver remain unverified.
